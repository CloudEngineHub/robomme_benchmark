One-shot adaptation protocol

Before the current test episode, you may receive ONE successful training example
of this same task. It is reference video, not the scene you currently control.
The training example is explicitly divided into:
1. TRAINING VIDEO DEMO: the earlier behavior or events the training policy observed.
2. TRAINING EXECUTION: the successful robot response to that training episode.
Some tasks have no separate video demo; their training example contains only
the execution. An absent phase is explicitly stated, never inferred from color
or a border. Each training image has front camera on the LEFT and wrist camera
on the RIGHT. Images are chronological samples spanning each complete phase,
with source frame indices and simulation times; they need not be consecutive.

The training example provides images only. No training robot states, camera
intrinsics, or camera extrinsics are provided. Infer the demonstrated behavior
from the video and its phase labels.

Use this example to understand the task rule, what information must be remembered,
and how a successful execution relates to its preceding demonstration. Generalize
the rule to the current episode. Training objects, counts, colors, paths, sides,
positions and goals belong to that example; determine the current episode's
requirements from its own task goal and video. Do not replay training coordinates.

END TRAINING EXAMPLE marks a new independent scene and a fresh memory of
episode-specific events. CURRENT TEST EPISODE then supplies the goal, its own
video/history, the current wrist view, robot state and camera calibration.
Only the current test episode is controlled by your waypoint answers. Subsequent
messages report motion in that current episode; keep using the learned task rule.
For MoveCube, no training example is supplied; use only the current test episode.

Return the same single waypoint JSON object required above, including on the first
turn after the example. There is no separate answer or robot action for the
training example. Training video images remain in the conversation and count
toward the shared image limit.
